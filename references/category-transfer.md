## Transfer sequence

Translate in five layers:

1. **Surface:** what literally happens in the source content.
2. **Tension:** what the audience is waiting to resolve.
3. **Psychology:** why the resolution matters socially or personally.
4. **Proof:** what makes the claim believable on screen.
5. **Target expression:** how the target product can reproduce the mechanism honestly.

Example:

```text
Surface: Foreigner attempts to order food using five local words.
Tension: Can limited knowledge complete a real task?
Psychology: competence fantasy + correction impulse + local identity.
Proof: the order is understood and arrives.
Target software expression: Give an AI browser one instruction and show whether it completes a real workflow.
```

## Product-family lenses

### Software and AI products

Prioritize:

- task completion proof;
- old versus new workflow;
- time saved;
- failure and recovery;
- user control, privacy, and trust;
- “one instruction” or “one session” challenges;
- comment-suggested use cases.

Useful analogous categories include cooking challenges, language challenges, makeover before/after, expert tool demonstrations, and behind-the-scenes production.

### B2B and professional services

Prioritize:

- decision risk reduction;
- process transparency;
- cost of inaction;
- diagnostic expertise;
- stakeholder disagreement;
- implementation reality;
- customer proof with credible boundaries.

Useful analogous categories include medical explainers, investigative reporting, repair diagnostics, financial breakdowns, and expert teardown content.

### Consumer products

Prioritize:

- sensory or visible proof;
- routine integration;
- comparison and trade-offs;
- first use;
- durability or stress tests;
- identity and gifting;
- user-generated modifications.

Useful analogous categories include food tasting, fashion styling, sports testing, home improvement, and budget challenges.

### Places, tourism, culture, and personal IP

Prioritize:

- outsider misconception and local correction;
- ordinary-person stories;
- relationship continuity;
- language learning;
- identity participation;
- stereotype reversal with evidence;
- behind-the-scenes professional access.

Avoid treating local people as scenery or using one individual as a national representative.

### Education and knowledge products

Prioritize:

- before/after understanding;
- misconception correction;
- field application;
- learner progress arcs;
- expert method made visible;
- useful frameworks worth saving;
- questions that invite self-diagnosis.

## Transfer test

Before adopting a mechanism, ask:

1. Does the target audience experience the same tension?
2. Can the target product provide equally visible proof?
3. Does the mechanism fit the brand's authority and tone?
4. Is the production repeatable under actual constraints?
5. Is the audience action aligned with the business objective?
6. What cultural, regulatory, or platform assumption changes?
7. Would the idea still work without imitation of the source creator's identity?

If the answer to 1, 2, or 4 is no, reject the transfer.

## Common failed transfers

- copying a viral topic without its underlying tension;
- copying an exaggerated reaction into a high-trust professional brand;
- copying street spontaneity where consent or permits are constrained;
- copying a large creator's production without their access or team;
- copying national-pride content that damages credibility with another audience;
- copying a conversion format when the current objective is awareness;
- importing platform pacing without adapting profile, caption, or follow behavior.

The correct unit of imitation is a truthful mechanism the target can repeatedly prove.
