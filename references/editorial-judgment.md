# Editorial Judgment

Use this reference when selecting benchmarks, defining recurring content, or protecting trust and aesthetic consistency. Optimize for the intended audience relationship, not only attention.

## Replace the default attention-grab mindset

Use **attention entry** as the neutral term for the first frame or line. Choose a mode intentionally:

| Mode | Best use | Evidence of quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct proof | The result is visually or behaviorally strong | The audience can judge the outcome without inflated claims |
| Narrative tension | Process and uncertainty genuinely matter | The resolution is real, not manufactured withholding |
| Quiet authority | Expertise, trust, or premium restraint matters | Precise observation, useful detail, and calm confidence |
| Founder thinking | The reasoning process is differentiated | Honest trade-offs, decisions, and learning rather than founder mythology |
| Documentary observation | Reality is more interesting than performance | Specific human detail, context, consent, and minimal staging |
| Community participation | Audience input can alter the next action | Comments create a real next test, response, or product use case |
| Search utility | The audience has a concrete recurring question | The content resolves the task clearly and is worth saving |
| Trust repair | Objections, limits, or controversy block adoption | Transparent boundaries, failure modes, controls, and corrective action |

## Editorial acceptance test

For every proposed recurring series, answer:

- What truthful value earns the first seconds of attention?
- Would the idea survive without exaggeration, hidden information, or a fake reaction?
- Is the creator or brand qualified to make the claim?
- Does the proof respect consent, privacy, and context?
- Would repeated publication strengthen the desired identity?
- Does the format leave room for nuance, failure, and correction?
- Is it recognizable without becoming formulaic?
- What business or relationship outcome matters besides views?

## Reject manipulative defaults

Do not default to:

- “you won't believe” or “nobody tells you” framing;
- fake shock, failure, conflict, or before/after states;
- artificial countdowns or unsupported urgency;
- exaggerated contrasts that erase trade-offs;
- keyword-comment bait whose promised value is thin or unavailable;
- local people used as scenery, props, or representatives of an entire culture;
- claims that the product, creator, or journalist cannot substantiate;
- copying a creator's persona when only the underlying mechanism transfers.

A high-performing example may still be rejected for brand acceptability. Record why.

## Evaluate beyond attention

Match metrics to the chosen mode:

- direct proof: qualified clicks, activation, or proof comprehension;
- quiet authority: saves, return viewers, credible comments, profile depth;
- documentary observation: meaningful shares, story responses, trust signals;
- search utility: search discovery, saves, completion, downstream questions;
- community participation: useful comments converted into follow-up actions;
- trust repair: objection resolution, sentiment shift, support reduction, or safer activation.

Views can diagnose distribution; they do not automatically validate positioning, trust, product intent, or commercial value.
