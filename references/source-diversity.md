# Source Diversity and Bounded Serendipity

Use this reference when evidence is thin, culturally concentrated, multilingual, or overly consistent with the starting hypothesis.

## Source-diversity test

Before claiming a pattern, inspect diversity across the dimensions that matter:

- platform and community;
- audience segment and buyer status;
- supportive, skeptical, hostile, and neutral viewpoints;
- creator, commenter, customer, non-customer, and expert roles;
- market, language, and cultural context;
- recent and relevant evergreen time windows;
- behavioral, self-reported, authoritative, and commercial source types.

Do not add diversity mechanically. Select dimensions that could plausibly reverse the decision.

## Language Lens Matrix

Track non-default-language branches:

| Field | Required note |
| --- | --- |
| Language/market | Source language and audience context |
| Research purpose | What bias, gap, or decision this lens tests |
| Search terms | Native terms used, not only machine-translated English keywords |
| Original verified | Whether the agent opened and checked the original source |
| Translation risk | Idiom, sentiment, politics, sarcasm, platform slang, or terminology risk |
| New finding | What this lens adds or contradicts |
| Decision impact | Include, exclude, narrow, or test |

Use machine translation for discovery, not invisible certainty. Preserve the original phrase when meaning matters, provide a careful paraphrase, and seek native review for sensitive claims.

## Mandatory multilingual trigger

Run a language lens as a core evidence branch, not optional serendipity, when any of these conditions holds:

- relevant high-signal examples or counterevidence are concentrated in another language;
- the product, creator, team, or cultural mechanism spans more than one language market;
- English retrieval is thin while another ecosystem contains visible behavior;
- the decision depends on native vocabulary, humor, identity, trust, or cultural correction.

Choose languages from where decision-relevant evidence exists, not from the report language. Chinese creator evidence can validate a hook, production mechanic, or comment dynamic, but it does not by itself prove demand among US or European users. Record separately: **mechanism transferable**, **target-market demand verified**, and **cultural adaptation required**.

## Bounded serendipity branch

Reserve at most 10%–15% of the research budget when optional exploration could create non-obvious value. This cap does not apply to a language branch triggered by the mandatory rule above. Choose one branch:

- an adjacent audience behaving similarly for a different reason;
- a category solving the same emotional tension with another format;
- a non-default language that may expose invisible evidence;
- a platform where the behavior appears in another context;
- a counterexample that the main query would not retrieve.

Record:

```text
Branch question:
Why it is outside the main path:
Budget/time cap:
Sources and language:
Unexpected finding:
Decision it could change:
Included in main evidence: yes/no, with reason
```

Do not require the branch to produce a conclusion. Promote a finding into the main evidence chain only when it has a direct source, survives contradiction testing, and changes a decision, mechanism, risk, or experiment.

## Thin-evidence behavior

When evidence remains thin:

- say what was searched and inaccessible;
- avoid prevalence, consensus, and demographic claims;
- preserve single examples as cases, not market truths;
- convert the uncertainty into a cheap test when safe;
- increase human or expert review for cultural and reputation risk;
- stop when more open-web searching repeats the same weak sources.
